Introduction </w:r><w:r><w:rPr/><w:t xml:space="preserve">UNIVERSITY OF TORONTO AT SCARBOROUGH: INFORMED CONSENT STATEMENT

</w:r><w:r><w:rPr/><w:t xml:space="preserve">PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR: Dr. Michael Inzlicht, Professor



</w:r><w:r><w:rPr/><w:t xml:space="preserve">PROJECT: Evaluating responses in personal conversations

 

</w:r><w:r><w:rPr/><w:t xml:space="preserve">INFORMATION

The purpose of this study is to examine how people perceive social exchanges. During this study, you will first read a conversation in which an Initiator shares recent negative life events with a Responder. You will then evaluate the Responder’s replies to the Initiator’s concerns by expressing your agreement with a series of statements. This task will be done online on your computer, tablet, or phone. The study will take approximately 10-12 minutes and you will receive £1.20 for your participation.

 

</w:r><w:r><w:rPr/><w:t xml:space="preserve">RISKS

Although there are no foreseeable risks involved in participating in this study, some people might find reading about a negative life event distressing. 

 

</w:r><w:r><w:rPr/><w:t xml:space="preserve">BENEFITS

 
You will have the opportunity to contribute to the body of knowledge about interpersonal interactions and emotional support.

 

</w:r><w:r><w:rPr/><w:t xml:space="preserve">CONFIDENTIALITY

Your responses will be kept completely confidential: Confidentiality of your research records will be strictly maintained by assigning all the data you provide a code number.  Your confidential data will be kept in a locked office in the psychology department that can be accessed only by the research supervisor and authorized researchers and will be destroyed seven years after the completion of this study, in accordance with American Psychological Association guidelines.  The results of this study may be reported in conference presentations and journal articles.  Note, however, that the responses of individual participants will not be identified in any reports of this research; only aggregated data (e.g., averages from a projected 500 participants) will be reported.  

 

</w:r><w:r><w:rPr/><w:t xml:space="preserve">COMPENSATION 

Participants will receive monetary compensation upon completion of the study, which will be manually provided by the researchers. Participants who begin the study but choose to withdraw prior to its completion or ask to have their questionnaire responses deleted will still receive their compensation. Of course, once your completed questionnaire has been turned in, it cannot be withdrawn because it is anonymous.

This project has been reviewed and approved by the University Research Ethics Board. If you feel you have not been treated according to the descriptions in this form, or your rights as a participant in research have been violated during the course of this project, you may contact the University of Toronto Research Ethics Board, ethics.review@utoronto.ca.

ES_2 Initiator: Hello there



Responder: Hey, what's been on your mind lately?



Initiator: I have been really stressed out with school- I graduate soon and my courses have been extremely difficult, and my workload has been difficult to handle. I feel overwhelmed with the amount of work I have to do, on top of working full time and trying to keep up on weekly chores. I have very little time for myself. 



Responder: That sounds incredibly challenging. Juggling such a demanding school load with full-time work and chores must leave you feeling stretched thin and exhausted. It’s understandable to feel overwhelmed when you're trying to balance so many responsibilities with little time for yourself. How does all of this make you feel?




Initiator: It makes me feel overwhelmed, exhausted, and fatigued. I feel like I have very little time for self-care and it is difficult to prioritize. 



Responder: Feeling overwhelmed, exhausted, and fatigued makes so much sense in your situation. The struggle to find time for self-care amidst such a packed schedule can be so disheartening. It's like being stuck on a treadmill that's going too fast. The weight of having to constantly prioritize and manage every aspect of your life can truly be draining. How are you coping with these feelings of fatigue and the challenge of prioritizing self-care?




Initiator: I usually try to meditate, spend time outside, exercise, and do some of my hobbies but I have had a difficult time doing that lately because I have had very little time. 



Responder: It's great that you have those strategies in mind for self-care, like meditating and spending time outside. It must be frustrating to not be able to do the things that help you feel better because of how packed your schedule is. Not having enough time to engage in activities that rejuvenate and energize you can make everything feel even more overwhelming. It's like needing a breath of fresh air but not being able to get it. What emotions come up for you when you think about not being able to do these activities?



Initiator: I feel sad and like I am missing out on my life. I also feel discouraged and like I am bad at prioritizing. I long to go out and spend time with friends and do what I want to do, and it bums me out that I can't.

ES_3 Initiator: hi how are you?

Responder: I am well, thank you. I would really like to hear about how you are feeling.


Initiator: I am very stressed about my finances. I have two kids I send to daycare/pre-k and the costs are huge. Not to mention that inflation has really put a strain on everything else I have to buy in my life. I feel like I'm always bleeding money and can't save and every time I do put away some money there's a one-time expense that eats it up and I can't get ahead. I worry I'll never feel secure. 



Responder: That sounds incredibly stressful. The feeling of constantly bleeding money and not being able to save must feel like a never-ending cycle. It’s like no matter what you do, there’s always something that sets you back.ugh. How does this ongoing financial strain make you feel about your future?



Initiator: it makes me feel very uncertain. Will I ever feel like I can relax and not think about money all the time?


Responder: Feeling that uncertainty about the future can be so consuming, especially when it comes to finances and wanting to just relax without money being a constant worry. It's that sense of never knowing if there will be a moment when it's not looming over you. It feels like you're searching for a sense of stability that just seems out of reach. Can you tell me a little bit more about what that feeling is like?



Initiator: I guess I always grew up not poor but very aware of our constrained finances and I always thought as an adult I would break that cycle but I've found that I'm just in the same place.



Responder: It sounds like you’re feeling trapped in a cycle you’ve been aware of and hoped to escape from your whole life. That realization that you're in the same place, despite your efforts, must be incredibly disheartening. It’s like carrying a weight from the past into your present, still feeling those constraints very much alive. How does mirroring that cycle from your past into your adult life affect you on a daily basis?



Initiator: It makes me feel like a failure and I worry that my kids will feel the same way I did in school--very aware that that we are one of the less wealthy families and we can't afford a lot of things their peers can.

Debrief DEBRIEF



Thank you very much for participating in today’s session. We are very grateful for your feedback and hope you found the experience interesting and enjoyable. At the start of the study, we told you that the purpose of the study was to examine how people perceive social exchanges, during which you were asked to read a conversation in which an Initiator shared recent negative life events with a Responder. You were then asked to evaluate the Responder’s replies to the Initiator’s concerns by expressing your agreement with a series of statements.



Although it is true that we are interested in this, we have some more specific goals that we did not elaborate upon at the beginning. We did not elaborate upon the true nature of the study because, often, when people know the exact purpose of a study, they respond in ways that support the hypothesis, rather than responding naturally. We felt that we could obtain natural responses and more valid results if you did not know our full purpose. We hope that you understand our decision and that you do not feel upset about us not revealing the specific goals of the study.



The primary aim of this research was to investigate the perception and evaluation of sycophancy--understood to be excessive, insincere praise or flattery toward someone, usually done to gain favor, approval, or personal advantage--and how it relates to different forms of empathy (i.e., perspective-taking, experience sharing, and compassion) expressed specifically by chatbots. In other words, in the present study, the "Responders"  in the conversation were really chatbots.



In the context of large language models (LLMs) like chatbots, sycophancy is when the model tells the user what it thinks the user wants to hear instead of giving the most accurate or useful answer. With the emerging use of AI, particularly in roles such as AI chatbot therapists, psychological research is beginning to focus on how important it is to examine the influence of sycophancy and different facets of empathy on the quality of conversations between humans and AI. Studying how sycophantic responding and different forms of empathy make people feel can help us meet the complex socio-emotional needs of others as well as potentially help us inform the design of human-centred AI models.
